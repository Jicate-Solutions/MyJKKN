PDE Pilot Cohort — Selection Framework

Selecting the first 30 coordinators for white-glove rollout
Tier 6 · T6.2 Audience: Director (Omm) Decision owner: Director Pace cap: 30 / 60 days

01 Why a pilot cohort

Quality over speed for the first cohort. Get 30 right; the next 60 are easier.

The PDE rollout is not a feature flip — every onboarded coordinator triggers downstream load: faculty validator bandwidth (each submission needs validation against the 7-category rubric), rubric calibration (early validations expose where rubric language is ambiguous and needs editing via /admin/pde/rubrics/*), and bridge audit overhead (legacy artifacts must be mapped to the new capability framework, dry-run reviewed, then applied).

A pilot cohort capped at 30 lets us tune all three loops before scaling. The faculty validators we lean on for cohort 1 become the calibration anchors for cohorts 2–4. Rubric edits made during pilot ship to everyone downstream. Bridge templates refined during pilot become the default for the 200+ coordinators behind them.

Strategic bet
The 60% active-use lock (Director, 2026-05-11) is measured at 90 days. Pilot cohort completion at day 60 gives a 30-day buffer to course-correct before the lock is evaluated.

02 Pace-cap rationale

The cohort size of 30 is enforced by platform policy pde.rollout.pace_cap_coordinators_per_60d (default: 30), consumed by pde-pace-cap-service (T2.1). Any onboarding request that would push the rolling 60-day count above the cap is blocked at /admin/pde/coordinators with a policy-pointing error message. Super-admin override exists but is logged.

Why 30 (and not 50 or 100)

  • Faculty validation throughput: assume 4 validators × 5 demonstrations/week × 60 days ≈ 240 validations. At 8 demonstrations per pilot coordinator, that's 30 coordinators worth of validation capacity with zero slack for re-validation cycles.
  • Onboarding meeting overhead: 30 × 60-minute 1:1 onboarding meetings = 30 hours over 60 days (roughly 1 hour/business day). Director-led; not delegable in cohort 1.
  • Rubric calibration loop: 30 coordinators surfaces enough discipline diversity (across 8 colleges) to expose rubric edge cases without overwhelming the editor cadence.
Policy adjustment
Pace cap is policy-driven. If cohort 1 metrics warrant faster expansion, edit the policy via /admin/pde/policies — no code change. The cap then re-evaluates on the next onboarding request.

03 Selection criteria

Five weighted criteria. Director can re-weight — totals must sum to 100%.

Each candidate coordinator is scored 0–10 on each criterion. Weighted sum (0–10 scale) gives priority rank. Suggested threshold for cohort 1 inclusion: weighted score ≥ 7.0. Director adjusts.

1. Faculty engagement history

40%

Has this coordinator already attested learner work in the legacy system? This is the single strongest predictor of pilot success — coordinators with attestation history understand the validator-coordinator-learner triangle and won't need foundational onboarding.

Scoring: 0 = no attestation history. 5 = sporadic attestations (1–5 in past 12 months). 10 = systematic attester (20+ attestations, faculty-validator relationship already established).

2. Institution diversity

20%

Cover all 8 colleges proportionally. A pilot dominated by one college (e.g., Engineering) won't surface cross-discipline rubric gaps. Per-college target counts are in Section 4.

Scoring: per-college quota — coordinator scores 10 if their college is under-quota, 5 if at quota, 0 if over-quota. Reweighs as cohort fills.

3. Existing PDE capability bridge potential

15%

Coordinator has high-value legacy capabilities that bridge cleanly to the new 7-category framework. High-bridge coordinators help calibrate the bridge service (T6.3) and produce demonstrations early because their legacy artifacts pre-populate evidence pools.

Scoring: 0 = no legacy artifacts. 5 = legacy artifacts exist but require manual mapping. 10 = legacy artifacts map 1:1 to PDE capabilities via existing bridge templates.

4. Discipline coverage

15%

Ensure the 7-category framework gets piloted across disciplines. Two coordinators from the same discipline produce correlated demonstrations and don't expose rubric ambiguity.

Scoring: 10 if discipline is under-represented in current cohort selection, 5 if at parity, 0 if over-represented.

5. Operational stability

10%

Coordinator has been in role > 6 months and has no pending separation, transfer, or extended leave. A coordinator who departs mid-pilot wastes validator capacity and produces an incomplete signal.

Scoring: 0 = pending separation or < 3 months in role. 5 = 3–6 months in role, stable. 10 = > 6 months in role, no flags.

05 Onboarding flow — 60-day cadence

From "added to platform" to "full autonomy" in 60 days, paced to match validator capacity.

  • Day 0 — Onboarding triggered Coordinator added via /admin/pde/coordinators. Pace-cap check (T2.1) confirms cohort slot available. Onboarding log row created (pde_coordinator_onboarding_log). Coordinator receives welcome email (per comms kit) with link to faculty playbook and onboarding meeting calendar.
  • Day 1–7 — Onboarding meeting + setup 60-minute 1:1 with Director (or designate). Walk through 7-category framework, demo the demonstration submission flow, introduce assigned faculty validator. Set expectation: "5 shadowed validations in the next 7 days, then independent."
  • Day 8–14 — Shadow 5 validations Coordinator observes their validator rate 5 learner demonstrations. Discusses each rating verbally. Surfaces rubric ambiguity — flagged for review at /admin/pde/rubrics.
  • Day 15–30 — Independent validation, weekly check-in Coordinator rates demonstrations independently. Validator reviews ratings async; weekly 15-min check-in resolves disputes. Coordinator submits 1–2 of their own demonstrations as learner (closes the loop — coordinator experiences both sides of the rubric).
  • Day 30–60 — Full autonomy, KPI tracking Coordinator operates independently. Activity feeds T6.4 KPI dashboard (demonstrations validated, demonstrations submitted, bridge runs triggered). No mandatory check-ins. Director reviews dashboard weekly for anomalies.
  • Day 60 — KPI review + cohort 2 gate Per-coordinator KPI review. Per-cohort metrics evaluated against Section 6 thresholds. If active-use ratio ≥ 40%, cohort 2 spawn approved (pace-cap rolls forward 60 days). If < 40%, pause + retro before cohort 2.
Why 60 days (not 30 or 90)
30 days is too short — coordinator hasn't seen a full grading cycle. 90 days delays cohort 2 unnecessarily and burns runway on the 90-day active-use lock. 60 days matches the pace-cap window (pace_cap_coordinators_per_60d) so cohort and policy clock are aligned.

07 Risk mitigation

Four risks ranked by likelihood × blast radius. Each has a pre-committed mitigation.

Risk 1 · High likelihood
Faculty resistance — perceived as "more work"

Coordinators see PDE as an added grading layer on top of existing attestation duties. Without explicit framing, they passively de-prioritize the pilot.

Mitigation: Comms kit (owned by Agent AJ) explicitly frames pilot as "no extra grading — replaces the legacy attestation step". Onboarding meeting caps at 60 minutes (no recurring meeting load). Faculty playbook (owned by Agent AI) shows the time-savings math vs legacy flow.
Risk 2 · Medium likelihood
Rubric ambiguity blocks validation

Coordinator and validator disagree on rubric interpretation. Demonstrations stall in review. Validator escalations spike.

Mitigation: Rubric editor live at /admin/pde/rubrics/* from day 0. Weekly rubric-review session during pilot — Director + 2 senior validators triage ambiguity reports + commit edits. Edits ship to all coordinators within 24h.
Risk 3 · Medium likelihood
Bridge audit reveals legacy artifact gaps

T6.3 bridge service maps legacy artifacts to new framework. For some coordinators the mapping is incomplete (artifacts don't cleanly bridge), leaving them with no pre-populated evidence and a cold start.

Mitigation: Dry-run bridge for every cohort 1 candidate before scoring them on Section 3 criterion 3. Coordinators with poor bridge map drop down the ranking — not excluded, but back-loaded into cohort 2 once bridge templates improve.
Risk 4 · Low likelihood, high impact
Pace-cap blocks high-priority add

Mid-cohort, a strategically important coordinator (e.g., new department head) needs onboarding but cap is full. Hard block creates political tension.

Mitigation: Super-admin override is already implemented in T2.1 (logged, reversible). Director-only. Use sparingly — every override beyond +2 over cap requires updating the policy value rather than overriding, to keep the policy honest.
