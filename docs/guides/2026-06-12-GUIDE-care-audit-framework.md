# JKKN CARE Audit Framework

**A universal engagement audit for any JKKN initiative**
Version 1.0 · June 2026 · #JKKN100

---

## Purpose

The CARE Audit measures whether any JKKN initiative — a course, an event, a platform, a process, a recognition program — delivers **Clarity, Appreciation, Recognition, and Empowerment** to the people it serves (Learners, Learning Facilitators, staff, or external stakeholders).

It exists because engagement failures are rarely content failures. Initiatives stall when people don't understand them (Clarity), feel invisible inside them (Appreciation), see no path to standing out (Recognition), or have no control within them (Empowerment).

**Alignment:** CARE operationalizes "Humans as Principals, AI as Agents" at the experience level — Principals require clarity to direct, and agency to act.

---

## How to Use

1. **Define the unit of audit.** One initiative, one audience. ("MyJKKN attendance module — for Learning Facilitators." "B.Sc. Microbiology Semester 1 — for Learners.")
2. **Score all 20 items** (5 per pillar) on the 0–4 scale below. Score what *exists and is verifiable*, not what is intended.
3. **Calculate pillar scores** (sum of 5 items, max 20) and the **CARE Index** (sum of all, max 80).
4. **Apply the gap rules** and write one corrective move per flagged pillar.
5. **Re-audit** after 90 days or one initiative cycle, whichever is shorter.

**Who scores:** Ideally two scorers — one initiative owner, one participant representative. A gap of more than 1 point on any item between scorers is itself a Clarity finding.

---

## Scoring Scale (all items)

| Score | Anchor |
|-------|--------|
| **0** | Absent — does not exist in any form |
| **1** | Informal — depends entirely on individual habit; no system |
| **2** | Partial — exists in a system but inconsistently applied or not visible to participants |
| **3** | Systematic — built into the workflow, applied consistently, visible |
| **4** | Systematic + Measured — consistent, visible, and tracked with data that someone reviews |

The 0–4 jump is deliberate: the difference between a 2 and a 3 is *system vs. habit* — the variance JKKN engineers out everywhere else.

## Pillar C — Clarity

*Does every participant know what this is, what success looks like, and why it matters?*

| # | Audit Item | Evidence to look for |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| C1 | **One-sentence purpose.** A first-time participant can state what this initiative is for after a single exposure. | Written purpose statement; spot-check 3 participants |
| C2 | **Explicit success criteria.** Expected outcomes, rubrics, or completion standards are published *before* participation begins. | Outcome contract, rubric, SLA, checklist |
| C3 | **Unambiguous roles.** Every participant knows what they do, what others do, and who decides. | RACI or role statements; absence of "who handles this?" messages in Google Chat |
| C4 | **Visible "why it matters."** The link to the participant's own stake — placement, exam, career, recognition, mission — is communicated, not assumed. | Purpose-to-stake mapping in onboarding/launch material |
| C5 | **Current timelines and next actions.** Steps, deadlines, and what-happens-next are published and *up to date*. | Live schedule; last-updated date within current cycle |

---

## Pillar A — Appreciation

*Is effort acknowledged frequently, specifically, and informally — not just outcomes, formally?*

| # | Audit Item | Evidence to look for |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| A1 | **Frequent informal acknowledgment.** Effort is noticed at a regular cadence (weekly or per-milestone), not only at endpoints. | Chat spotlights, verbal rituals, facilitator habits with a defined cadence |
| A2 | **Process-specific feedback.** Praise names the behavior or approach ("your sampling method was rigorous"), not the person ("you're brilliant"). | Sample of actual feedback messages |
| A3 | **Fast feedback loops.** Time from submission/contribution to first acknowledgment is days, not weeks. | Median response time on assessments, proposals, messages |
| A4 | **Coverage of the median.** Appreciation reaches typical participants, not only top performers. | Distribution check: what % of participants received any acknowledgment this cycle? |
| A5 | **Two-way appreciation.** Participants have a sanctioned channel to appreciate facilitators/organizers, and it is used. | Feedback forms with appreciation field; peer-shoutout mechanism |

---

## Pillar R — Recognition

*Are there visible, attainable, distributed ways to be seen for progress and achievement?*

| # | Audit Item | Evidence to look for |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| R1 | **Defined recognition surfaces.** Named, scheduled mechanisms exist (showcases, tiers, badges, leaderboards, certificates). | Recognition calendar or tier definition document |
| R2 | **Progress recognition, not just peaks.** Improvement and consistency are recognized, not only wins and toppers. | Adoption tiers, streaks, most-improved categories |
| R3 | **Distribution beyond the top decile.** Recognition reaches a broad base; it is not tournament-only. | % of participants recognized per cycle (target: >25%) |
| R4 | **Public and attributable.** Recognition is visible to peers and names the person and the specific contribution. | Public channels, notice boards, #JKKN100 posts naming individuals |
| R5 | **Recognition feeds forward.** Being recognized leads somewhere — portfolio entry, LinkedIn artifact, NIF pipeline, role advancement. | Documented pathway from recognition to opportunity |

---

## Pillar E — Empowerment

*Do participants have real choices, real ownership, and real authority within the initiative?*

| # | Audit Item | Evidence to look for |
|---|-----------|---------------------|
| E1 | **Meaningful choice.** Participants choose at least one substantive dimension: topic, format, pacing, team, or tool. | Documented choice points in the initiative design |
| E2 | **Ownership of real output.** Participants produce something with their name on it that exists beyond the initiative (product, publication, event, dataset). | AI Production House model; output inventory |
| E3 | **Decision authority.** Some decisions are genuinely delegated — participants can decide without approval, within stated bounds. | Delegation matrix; examples of participant decisions that stood |
| E4 | **Tools as agents.** Participants are given capable tools (AI, platforms, budgets) and trusted to operate them as Principals. | Gemini/NotebookLM access with creation rights, not just consumption |
| E5 | **Voice changes the system.** Participant feedback has visibly altered the initiative at least once per cycle, and the change was announced as such. | "You said, we changed" log |

## Scoring & Interpretation

### Pillar Scores (per pillar, max 20)

| Range | Rating | Meaning |
|-------|--------|---------|
| 17–20 | **Exemplary** | Systematic and measured; share as institutional model |
| 13–16 | **Established** | Systematic but with measurement or coverage gaps |
| 8–12 | **Habit-dependent** | Exists but rides on individuals; will not survive personnel change |
| 0–7 | **Critical gap** | Effectively absent; predictable engagement failure point |

### CARE Index (max 80)

| Range | Verdict |
|-------|---------|
| 65–80 | Engagement-ready initiative |
| 48–64 | Sound core, targeted fixes needed |
| 30–47 | Redesign the weak pillars before scaling |
| < 30 | Do not scale; rebuild the experience layer |

### Gap Rules (apply regardless of total)

1. **Floor rule:** Any pillar < 8 must produce a corrective move with an owner and a date, even if the CARE Index is high. A 70/80 with Appreciation at 5 is still an appreciation failure.
2. **Median rule:** If A4 or R3 scores ≤ 1, the initiative serves its top decile only. Flag for the NIRF lens — institutional outcomes are won at the median.
3. **Variance rule:** If two scorers differ by ≥ 2 points on any item, treat it as a C-pillar finding (the system is not legible) before debating whose score is right.
4. **System rule:** Items scoring 1 ("informal") are higher risk than items scoring 0 — an absent system can be built; a habit-dependent one creates the illusion of coverage.

## Corrective Move Template

For every flagged pillar, complete one line:

> **Pillar:** ___ · **Lowest item:** ___ · **Move (one sentence):** ___ · **Owner:** ___ · **Mechanism it lives in (CAMU / MyJKKN / Google Chat / calendar):** ___ · **Re-audit date:** ___

A corrective move that does not name a *mechanism* defaults to habit — which is a score of 1, the score the audit exists to eliminate.

## Audit Cadence by Initiative Type

| Initiative type | When to audit |
|----------------|---------------|
| Courses / academic programs | Week 3 of semester (early enough to fix), then end of semester |
| Platforms (MyJKKN modules, CAMU workflows) | Before scale-up of any module; annually thereafter |
| Events & programs (Yi, NIF, #JKKN100) | At design stage (pre-mortem), within 2 weeks post-event |
| Processes (procurement, onboarding, reporting) | At rollout; after any ownership change |
| Recognition / adoption frameworks (NotebookLM tiers) | Quarterly distribution check (A4/R3 only) |

## Notes on Honest Scoring

- Score the participant's experience, not the owner's intention. If it isn't visible to a Learner, it doesn't exist for a Learner.
- CARE is a sensitivity framework, not a generational science. These are good design principles for any cohort; Gen Z Learners simply disengage faster and more visibly when they are absent.
- The audit's most common failure mode is scoring documents instead of behavior. A published rubric nobody reads is a 2, not a 4 — "visible" means *seen*, and "measured" means *someone reviews the data*.
